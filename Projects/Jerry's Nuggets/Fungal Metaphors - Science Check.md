# Fungal Metaphors - Science Check

Working notes for [[Fabulous Fungal Metaphors]], prepared with Claude on 11 Nov 2026. One entry per nugget: what is safe to say, and where to be careful.

Each line is marked **[looked up]** (checked against a source that night; links at the bottom) or **[from memory]** (Claude's general knowledge, not yet checked against a source). Treat the second kind as probably right and worth a quick confirmation before posting.

The three places where the popular story runs ahead of the science are the Wood Wide Web, mycoremediation, and how psilocybin works. Those entries matter most.

## Hyphae Connect

- **[from memory]** A hypha is a thread-like filament, a few thousandths of a millimeter wide. Hyphae grow at their tips, branch, and fuse with each other. The resulting web is called a mycelium.
- **[from memory]** Careful: not every fungus is made of hyphae. Yeasts are single cells.

## Mycelia Are Nature's Internet

- **[from memory]** A mycelium moves water, nutrients and chemical signals from one part of itself to another, and reroutes around damage.
- **[from memory]** Careful: this is a metaphor, popularized by Paul Stamets. A mycelium is usually one organism talking to itself, not many parties on a shared network. Claims that fungi have a "language" of electrical spikes are speculative.

## Mycelia Externalize Their Digestion

- **[from memory]** Fungi release enzymes into whatever they are growing through, break food down outside their bodies, and then absorb the small molecules. Animals do the reverse: swallow first, digest inside. This one is solid textbook biology.

## Leaf-Cutter Ants Can't Digest Leaves

- **[looked up]** The ants do not eat the leaves. They chew them into a bed for a fungus, Leucoagaricus gongylophorus, and eat the fungus.
- **[looked up]** The fungus grows special swollen tips, called gongylidia, packed with fats and proteins, which the ants harvest.
- **[looked up]** Neither can live without the other. The fungus, grown alone, is quickly overrun by competitors.
- **[looked up]** A lovely detail: the fungus's enzymes pass through the ants undigested, and the ants deposit them on fresh leaf material, so the ants carry the fungus's digestion to where it is needed.
- **[from memory]** Careful: "can't digest leaves" is fair shorthand. Adult workers do drink some plant sap while cutting.

## Fungi are the Principal Decomposers in Ecological Systems

- **[from memory]** Fungi and bacteria together do most of the world's decomposing. Fungi are the main decomposers of wood, because certain fungi (white rot) are the only organisms that can fully break down lignin, the tough material that makes wood woody.
- **[from memory]** Careful: "principal" is true for wood and forest litter. In other settings bacteria do more.
- **[from memory]** Avoid the popular story that coal exists because fungi had not yet evolved to rot wood. Later research disputes it.

## Fungal Fixers - Mycoremediation

- **[looked up]** The same enzymes fungi use on lignin can break down pollutants, including petroleum hydrocarbons, pesticides and industrial dyes.
- **[looked up]** Careful: this is well demonstrated in the lab, with few trials at pilot scale. A 2019 review found little progress on commercial use for oil-contaminated soil. A 2026 review says it works best combined with other methods, not alone.
- **[from memory]** Careful: fungi do not destroy heavy metals. They can concentrate them, which moves the problem into the fungus.
- Safe framing: promising and real, not yet a proven clean-up technology at scale.

## Fungi Are Essential to Soil Health

- **[from memory]** Most land plants, roughly eight or nine species in ten, partner with fungi at their roots (mycorrhizae).
- **[from memory]** Fungal threads and their sticky by-products bind soil particles into crumbs, which helps soil hold water and carbon.
- **[from memory]** Careful: avoid naming "glomalin" as the glue. What it actually is has become a matter of debate.

## Mycelia Trade Nutrients in the Wood Wide Web

This nugget holds two claims. Split them.

**Claim one: the fungus-root trade. Solid.**

- **[looked up]** Fungi at the root tip gather water and nutrients for the plant, and the plant pays in sugars. Researchers call it mutualism.

**Claim two: trees linked by a shared fungal network, sharing food and warnings. Contested.**

- **[looked up]** The phrase "wood-wide web" dates from Nature in 1997. Suzanne Simard's work in Douglas fir forests made it famous.
- **[looked up]** In 2023, Justine Karst and two colleagues reviewed the field studies in Nature Ecology & Evolution. Their findings:
	- Shared networks exist, but too few forests have been mapped to say they are widespread.
	- For seedlings, being connected helped about as often as it hurt, and most often made no difference.
	- No peer-reviewed field study shows older trees sending warnings or extra food to young trees through the network.
- **[looked up]** They are not disputing that these fungi matter to forests. Simard has replied that critiques are a normal part of research.
- Safe framing: the trade between fungus and root is real; the forest-wide sharing network is a beautiful hypothesis that is still being tested. Saying so openly fits the disclaimer already on the page.

## The Matsutake's Peculiar Ecosystem

- **[from memory]** Source is Anna Tsing's book The Mushroom at the End of the World (2015). Matsutake partner with pine roots, do well in forests disturbed by people, and have resisted commercial cultivation.
- **[from memory]** Careful: the story that matsutake was the first thing to grow in Hiroshima after the bomb is told by Tsing as a story. Present it as lore.

## Magic Mushrooms

- **[from memory]** Psilocybin is converted in the body to psilocin, which acts on serotonin receptors.
- **[looked up]** The idea that it "drops the veils" has a scientific version: the REBUS model (Carhart-Harris and Friston, 2019), short for "relaxed beliefs under psychedelics". It proposes that psychedelics loosen the brain's high-level expectations, so more raw incoming information gets through and entrenched beliefs can be revised.
- **[looked up]** Careful: REBUS is a proposed model, not an established fact. "One leading model suggests" is the safe phrasing.
- Careful: legal status varies by place, and this is not medical advice.

## Mushrooms Are the Fruiting Bodies of Mycelium

- **[from memory]** A mushroom is the reproductive part. It appears briefly, releases spores, and is gone, while the mycelium below persists for years.
- **[from memory]** Careful: many fungi never make mushrooms at all.

## Fungi Can Be Huge

- **[looked up]** The "Humongous Fungus" is a single honey mushroom, Armillaria ostoyae, in the Malheur National Forest in eastern Oregon. It covers 2,385 acres (965 hectares).
- **[looked up]** Age estimates run from at least 2,400 years to as much as 8,650. The range is wide because it depends on the assumed growth rate.
- **[looked up]** Weight estimates run from 7,500 to 35,000 US tons.
- **[looked up]** Careful: "largest organism" depends on what counts as one organism, and some experts are no longer sure it holds the title.
- **[looked up]** Careful: it is not gentle. It is a root disease that kills trees.
