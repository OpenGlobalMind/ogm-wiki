# Ways We Give Away Our Thinking
 (draft) 
*Subhed.* 

**Beat 1 — Cold open, standing on the line.** Open in the keeper-line register. No throat-clearing: name that the decision is already being made, quietly, and that you've spent thirty years on the other side of it.

**Beats 2–3 — The four scenarios.** Four forces, each a convenience with a hidden cost. *(These four are locked in as the set; the phrasings below are drafts to tune against your own ear — the smooth-consensus one is closest to final because it sets up Video 4.)*

- **The unchecked summary.** The AI-generated summary of the meeting or the document becomes the record. Nobody checks it against the source, so whatever it quietly dropped is simply gone — and the org now remembers the summary, not the thing.  Are you using Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, Granola or Zoom's built-in AI meeting summaries?
- **The first draft that becomes final.** The AI first draft is good enough to ship, so it ships. But the draft was supposed to be where thinking *starts*. The revision that would have surfaced the real disagreement never happens.
- **The sawn-off ladder.** Juniors used to earn competence on the bottom rungs — the research memo, the rough model, the first pass. AI now does those rungs. The output is faster today; the senior people you needed ten years from now never got to climb. New Science Stuffing Parties, 
- **The smooth consensus.** AI answers arrive pre-averaged. The productive friction of two people who genuinely disagree — the thing that used to surface the real issue — gets replaced by text that reads plausible from every angle. *(This one plants the seed Video 4's provenance demo will grow.)*

**Beat 4 — The reframe.** "Notice: not one of these involves anyone doing anything wrong. Each one is a convenience, adopted for good reasons. That's what makes this hard — and what makes it a frontier rather than a scandal. The question isn't 'who screwed up.' It's: now that you can see these forces, how do you want to shape them?"

It's a natural creep. 

### Cognitive Offloading vs Cognitive Surrender

**Beat 5 — The credential, as afterthought.** "I've spent thirty years on the other side of this line — building an external memory of my own, 625,000 ideas, every link a decision I made and can stand behind. From over there, this boundary doesn't look scary. It looks like the most interesting place in the world to stand. Next week I'll take you across and show you around."

**Beat 6 — The toss-back.** "Which of these have you already spotted in your own organization? And which ones might be running that you haven't looked for yet? Tell me in the comments. I read them all."
